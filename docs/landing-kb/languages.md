# Languages the chat speaks

Yes — the agent answers in Greek as well as English, and in most other
languages a visitor is likely to write in.

## How it decides

The agent **mirrors the visitor's language**: it replies in whatever language
the visitor writes in, on the first message, without anyone configuring
anything. A visitor who opens in Greek gets Greek back; the next visitor
writing in English gets English. There is no per-language setup, no separate
agent per language, and no language switch for the visitor to find.

This is on by default for every agent on every plan.

## What it answers from

The language of the reply and the language of your source material are
separate things. Your knowledge base can be written entirely in English and
the agent will still answer a Greek question in Greek, working from that
English material — and the same the other way round.

Two honest caveats:

- The answer is only as good as the material behind it. A question your
  knowledge base does not cover is not answered by translating harder; the
  agent says it doesn't know and offers a human, in the visitor's language.
- Names, product terms and specific wording stay closest to how you wrote
  them. If a term matters to you in Greek — a service name, a legal phrase —
  put that wording in your knowledge base and the agent will use it.

## Quick answers to language questions

- **Can it answer in Greek?** Yes, and in English, and in most languages a
  visitor writes in.
- **Do I need a separate agent per language?** No. One agent handles them all.
- **Do I have to translate my knowledge base?** No. Add Greek wording only
  where the exact phrasing matters to you.
- **Which language do the transcripts and the leads dashboard show?** The
  conversation exactly as it happened, in the language it happened in.
